Introduction
Authors and management experts have defined research in various ways. 
Typically, research is said to commence with a question or a problem. It is described 
as the creation of new concepts, methodologies, and understandings through the 
development of new knowledge or the innovative application of existing knowledge. 
This process often involves synthesizing and analysing previous studies to produce 
new and groundbreaking results.
1.1 Research
Through research, we can identify solutions to problems by employing systematic 
and scientific methods. This may involve experimentation or innovation, with “risk” 
highlighting the inherent dangers of discovery. 
1.1.1 Definition and Objectives of Research
What is research?
Investigation might lead to analysis, and tests could be conducted to verify 
hypotheses. This work is distinct and unprecedented, offering new insights as a 
result. It could introduce a novel method or new data source, leading to significant 
breakthroughs or incremental improvements in current practices. Research is essential 
for progress, as it is fundamentally tied to “research and development.”
Slesinger & Stephenson, Encyclopaedia of Social Sciences:
“the manipulation of things, concepts or symbols for the purpose of generalizing to 
extend, correct or verify knowledge, whether that knowledge aids in the construction of 
theory or in the practice of an art”.
●
●
Redman & Mory: “Systematized effort to gain new knowledge”. It is an academic 
activity and should be used in a technical sense.
●
●
Clifford Woody: Research comprises “defining and redefining problems, 
formulating hypotheses or suggested solutions; collecting, organizing and 
evaluating data; making deductions and reaching conclusions; and finally, carefully 
testing the conclusions to determine whether they fit the formulated hypotheses”.

1.1.2: Characteristics of Research
● 
The research should concentrate on the most pressing issues.
● 
The investigation should be methodical. It emphasises the importance of following a 
structured procedure when conducting research.
● 
The research should follow a logical pattern. The scientific researcher cannot make 
much progress in any investigation without manipulating ideas logically.
● 
The study should be condensed. This means that a researcher’s findings should 
be made available to other researchers so that they don’t have to repeat the same 
research.
● 
The findings should be repeatable. This states that results from earlier studies have 
to be verifiable in new locations, environments, with fresh people, or at a different 
time.
● 
The research ought to be successful. The fact that a single research topic may spark 
the creation of several others is one of its most significant features.
● 
Action-oriented research is required. In other words, it should aim to find a solution 
that will allow its findings to be implemented.
● 
The research should take an integrated multidisciplinary approach, which means it 
will require research approaches from multiple disciplines.
● 
At all stages of the study, all parties involved (from policymakers to community 
members) should be invited to participate.
● 
The research has to have an easy-to-understand design and be conducted within a 
specific timeframe.
● 
As little money as feasible should be spent on the research.
● 
Formats that are most helpful to administrators, decision-makers, company managers, 
or community members should be used to deliver the study findings.

1.1.3 Differences b/w Research Methods and Research Methodology
Although the names sound similar, both Research methods and Research 
methodology are different, as explained below:
Research Methods
The various procedures, schemes, steps, and algorithms used in research are 
known as research methods. The term “research methods” refers to all of the methods 
used by a researcher during a research study. They’re primarily planned, scientific, and 
value-agnostic. Observations, theoretical procedures, experimental studies, numerical 
schemes, statistical approaches, and so on are all examples of these. We can use 
research methods to collect samples, data, and come up with a solution to a problem. 
Business and scientific research methods, in particular, demand explanations based on 
collected facts, measurements, and observations, rather than solely on reasoning. They 
only accept explanations that can be verified through experiments.
Research Methodology
A systematic approach to solving a problem is known as research methodology. It 
is a science that studies how research should be conducted. Research methodology 
is essentially the procedures by which researchers go about their work of describing, 
explaining, and predicting phenomena. It can also be defined as the study of methods 
for gaining knowledge. Its goal is to provide a research work plan.
1.2 Research Process
In the early decades human inquiry was primarily based on the examination of 
one’s own conscious thoughts and feelings that means the observation of any one and 
understanding through logical discussion to seek the truth. This process served as a 
well-established conceptual framework for comprehending the world and was accepted 
for a century. An essential component of the investigation process was the knowledge 
seeker. With time, this part was changed. The Scientific method introduced several 
major components in research procedures like Objectivity. 
1.2.1 Research Criteria and Research Process
Research Criteria:
● 
The goal of the study should be made very clear, and common principles should be 
applied.
● 
There should be enough description of the research process. in such a way as 
to preserve the integrity of the work previously completed while enabling another 
researcher to carry out the research for future development.
● 
To get unbiased outcomes, the procedural design of the research should be carefully 
prepared.
● 
Any shortcomings in the technique design should be fully disclosed by the researcher, 
along with an assessment of how they could affect the results.
● 
The analytic techniques employed should be suitable, and the data should be 
sufficiently analysed to demonstrate its relevance. Verification of the data’s legitimacy 
and dependability is necessary.

Research Process:
Planning must be done methodically at every step of the marketing research

greatest degree possible, time-scheduled. In order to examine theories or preconceived 
beliefs, marketing research employs the scientific method, which involves gathering and 
analysing data.
For reliable information that accurately reflects the scenario, marketing research 
should be carried out impartially. The study should be impartial and devoid of 
the personal or political prejudices of the researcher or management, even if the 
researcher’s research philosophy will always have an impact on the findings.
For instance, we could discover that the project’s scope is insufficient, that our 
topic is too wide and has to be limited, that there aren’t enough information resources 
accessible, or that the material we gather contradicts our thesis.
There are nine main steps of the research process that are followed at the time of 
designing a research project. They are as follows.
Step 1: Problem Definition
Step 2: Development of an Approach to the Problem
Step 3: Research Design Formulation
Step 4: Field Work or Data Collection
Step 5: Data Preparation and Analysis
Step 6: Report Preparation and Presentation
1.2.2: Approaches of Research: Deductive, Inductive, Quantitative 
and Qualitative
Deductive: Deductive approach starts with developing a hypothesis based on 
existing theory, and then prepares a research strategy for testing the hypothesis. To 
reason deductively is to go from the specific to the general. In many circumstances, 
a theory or case study that suggests a causal relationship or correlation may be 
accurate. The term “deductive approach” refers to a method of formulating hypotheses 
or drawing conclusions from theoretical ideas. The deductive method is concerned with 
drawing conclusions from statements or premises. By “testing against observations,” 
deductive reasoning begins with a pattern, whereas induction methodology “begins with 
observations and seeks to find a pattern within them.”
Benefits of Deductive:
● 
Probability to clarify the relationships between variables and concepts.
● 
Probability to measure concepts in a quantitative manner.
● 
Probability to generalize research findings to a certain extent.

had commenced.

patterns, resemblances and regularities are observed in order to reach conclusions or 
to generate theory.

evaluate the suggested investigation.
